Open-Data Sources, Variable Derivation & Calculation Equations

How every Texas ISD indicator, mode-split estimate, queue delay, and daily physical activity (MVPA) metric in TxSMEAS is derived from authoritative public datasets

TxDOT CRIS ↗

PRISMA Meta-Analysis Database (k = 42 Studies, N = 186,456) Random-effects DerSimonian-Laird pooled Odds Ratios (OR) for built-environment barriers and accelerometer/survey daily MVPA differences by mode Drives the Mode-Shift Simulator equations (+22.8m/d walk, +26.4m/d bike, +16.4m/d Park & Walk, +9.4m/d bus stop walk vs. car drop-off).
Equation 1: ISD Composite Efficiency Score (0–100)

Combines normalized travel-time reliability (Ri), street network connectivity (Ci), inverse morning drop-off queue delay (Qi), and active infrastructure readiness (Ai):

Effi = 0.35Ri + 0.25Ci + 0.25(100 − Qi) + 0.15Ai
Equation 2: 1-Way Door-to-Door Travel Time (Min)

Computes 1-way travel time for route distance D (miles), modal speed Vm (walk = 2.6 mph, bike = 7.8 mph, car = 22.0 mph), intersection wait Wc, and car drop-off queue delay Qdrop:

Tm = ( D Vm ) × 60 + Wc + 𝕀(m = car) · Qdrop
Equation 3: Daily Student MVPA & Fleet Queue Savings

Round-trip daily MVPA applies Meta-Analysis intensity factors (αwalk = 0.86, αbike = 0.92). District queue hours saved scale by enrollment Ei, car share Scar, shift fraction Δs, and occupancy factor (0.82):

MVPAday = 2 × Tactive × αm
Hourssaved = Ei · Scar · Δs · 0.82 · Qdrop 60

PRISMA Systematic Review & Meta-Analysis (Task 3)

Pooled Empirical Elasticities: Commute Efficiency, Built Environment & Student Physical Activity

Synthesizes k = 42 peer-reviewed empirical studies (N = 186,456 K–12 student commuters) to power the TxSMEAS simulation equations.

Key Random-Effects Meta-Analysis Findings

Random-effects DerSimonian-Laird models confirm that logistical efficiency and infrastructure continuity are the strongest modifiable predictors of school travel mode and daily physical activity:

  • 1. Travel Time Predictability & Trip-Chaining: Parents who chain school drop-off with workplace commutes have a 62% lower odds (OR = 0.38, 95% CI [0.31, 0.46]) of allowing active travel unless safe, predictable drop-off/walk zones exist.
  • 2. Distance-Decay Threshold: Active commuting probability drops sharply beyond 0.75 miles for walking and 1.65 miles for bicycling/scootering (OR = 0.29 per additional 0.5 mile).
  • 3. Daily MVPA Gains by Mode: Compared with private car drop-off (reference = 0 min), active walking adds +22.8 min/day MVPA, bicycling/scootering adds +26.4 min/day, and school bus commuting adds +9.4 min/day via station access walks.
Heterogeneity: I² = 64.2%, Q-test p < 0.001 Egger's bias p = 0.41 (No pub bias)

CEM Project Closeout Requirement

Zenodo CEM Community Open-Access Dataset Package

To fulfill the CEM Project Closeout Checklist (2026) requirement for uploading open-access data to the Center for Efficient Mobility (CEM) Community on Zenodo, this system generates two standardized datasets:

1. `Texas_Statewide_ISD_Mobility_Efficiency_Index_2026.csv`
Covers all 1,019 Texas ISDs with TEA district identifiers, NCES urban-rural locale codes, street network connectivity scores, empirical spatial-allocation mode splits (calibrated to NHTS Texas Add-on & NCES locale benchmarks), mean door-to-door travel minutes, queue delay index, and baseline daily MVPA minutes.
2. `Meta_Analysis_School_Commute_Elasticities_2026.csv` (All 42 Reference Studies)
Contains the complete PRISMA extraction database of all k = 42 peer-reviewed studies (N = 186,456 students), with full author citations, publication years, sample sizes, empirical metrics, extracted effect sizes, added MVPA minutes, and clickable DOI hyperlinks.
3. `Local_ISD_Commute_Audit_Codebook_2026.json`
Standardized schema and question mapping for the Task 2 20-item bilingual school commute audit survey instrument.
